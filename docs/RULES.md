# Game Rules

## Fantasy Chess Rules

Fantasy Chess follows traditional chess rules with a fantasy theme overlay. Each piece has been reimagined as a fantasy character while maintaining its original movement patterns.

## Piece Movement

### King (Royal Commander)
- Moves one square in any direction (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal)
- Special Ability: **Royal Command** - Can command nearby pieces

### Queen (Sorceress Queen)
- Moves any number of squares in any direction
- Special Ability: **Arcane Blast** - Most powerful piece on the board

### Rook (Tower Guardian)
- Moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically
- Special Ability: **Fortress Shield** - Protects allied pieces

### Bishop (Mystic Bishop)
- Moves any number of squares diagonally
- Special Ability: **Divine Light** - Illuminates the path

### Knight (Dragon Rider)
- Moves in an L-shape: 2 squares in one direction, 1 square perpendicular
- Can jump over other pieces
- Special Ability: **Leap of Faith** - Only piece that can jump

### Pawn (Warrior)
- Moves forward one square (or two squares from starting position)
- Captures diagonally forward one square
- Special Ability: **Shield Wall** - Forms defensive formations

## Special Moves

### Castling
A special move involving the King and Rook. The King moves two squares toward a Rook, and the Rook moves to the square the King crossed over. Requirements:
- Neither piece has moved before
- No pieces between them
- King is not in check
- King doesn't move through check

### En Passant
A special pawn capture. If an opponent's pawn moves two squares forward from its starting position and lands beside your pawn, you can capture it as if it had only moved one square.

### Promotion
When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board, it can be promoted to any other piece (except King), typically a Queen.

## Winning the Game

### Checkmate
The game ends when a King is in check and has no legal moves to escape. The player delivering checkmate wins.

### Stalemate
If a player has no legal moves and their King is not in check, the game is a draw.

### Draw Conditions
- Stalemate
- Threefold repetition (same position occurs three times)
- Fifty-move rule (no captures or pawn moves in 50 moves)
- Insufficient material (not enough pieces to checkmate)
- Agreement between players

## Basic Strategy Tips

1. **Control the Center**: Occupy and control the center squares (e4, d4, e5, d5)
2. **Develop Pieces**: Bring your pieces into play early in the game
3. **King Safety**: Castle early to protect your King
4. **Pawn Structure**: Maintain a solid pawn formation
5. **Piece Coordination**: Work your pieces together for attacks
6. **Think Ahead**: Calculate moves several turns in advance
7. **Trade Wisely**: Exchange pieces when it benefits your position

## Notation

### Algebraic Notation
- Files (columns): a-h (left to right)
- Ranks (rows): 1-8 (bottom to top from White's perspective)
- Piece symbols: K (King), Q (Queen), R (Rook), B (Bishop), N (Knight)
- Example: Nf3 (Knight moves to f3)
- Capture: x (e.g., Nxe5 - Knight captures on e5)
- Check: + (e.g., Qh5+)
- Checkmate: # (e.g., Qf7#)
- Castling kingside: O-O
- Castling queenside: O-O-O

## Getting Started

1. White always moves first
2. Players alternate turns
3. Only one piece can be moved per turn
4. Captured pieces are removed from the board
5. The game ends in checkmate, stalemate, or draw

Enjoy your magical chess adventure! ⚔️
